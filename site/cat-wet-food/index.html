Jelly vs Gravy Textures

Cats have strong texture opinions, and the jelly versus gravy divide is real. Jelly sets the meaty pieces in a light gel that keeps them moist and is easy to lap up, which suits cats that eat delicately and owners who prefer less mess. Gravy coats the pieces in a richer sauce that strong smelling cats find irresistible, though some cats lick the gravy and abandon the chunks. If you are unsure which your cat prefers, buy one pouch of each texture before committing to a box; the PKR 240 to 250 single pouch price makes experimenting cheap. Our jelly range includes the Beef and Poultry pouch, the Meaty Meals in Jelly, and the multi flavour 12 pack variety box, so jelly fans are well covered.

Hydration: Why Wet Food Matters

Cats evolved as desert animals with a weak thirst drive, and chronic low water intake is linked to urinary tract issues, especially in male cats. Wet food is typically 75 to 80 percent moisture against dry food's 10 percent, so a single daily pouch meaningfully lifts total water intake without any effort from you. This is the reason vets commonly recommend mixed feeding: dry kibble available for grazing plus one wet pouch a day. For cats that already show urinary sensitivity, increasing the wet proportion further is one of the simplest dietary steps you can take, alongside keeping water bowls clean, wide and away from the food bowl.

Serving and Safety Tips

Never serve a pouch that is puffed, leaking or smells off; compromised seals mean compromised food, and we replace any damaged pouches reported with a photo within 48 hours. Do not microwave pouches in their packaging; warm the food by standing the sealed pouch in warm water for a few minutes instead. Wash the bowl daily, because wet food residue grows bacteria fast in our climate. If your cat is new to wet food, start by mixing a spoonful into the dry kibble and increase gradually; most cats convert within a week once they catch the aroma.

Mixed Feeding Schedules That Work

The routine most owners sustain long term is simple: dry kibble available for daytime grazing, one wet pouch served fresh in the evening when you are home to remove leftovers. This gives the hydration benefit of wet food plus the dental and convenience benefits of dry, without doubling your budget. For a single adult cat this means roughly 40 to 50 grams of dry food plus one 85g pouch daily, adjusted to body condition; you should feel ribs under a light fat cover, not see them. Kittens and highly active cats need more total food, seniors often need slightly less. Whatever schedule you choose, keep it consistent, because cats are creatures of habit and regular mealtimes reduce begging, night time restlessness and digestive surprises.
